The Science of Sound

Sound is not only heard — it is felt. When precisely tuned frequencies move through the body, the nervous system responds in measurable ways.

Stress reduction

Sound therapy is studied for lowering cortisol and activating the parasympathetic nervous system — the state in which repair happens.

Brainwave states

Steady rhythms, plus two slightly different tones played one in each ear, are researched for guiding the mind toward alpha and theta states linked to clarity, memory and deep rest.

Vibration & the body

Putting the vibration directly against the body is researched as a way of applying therapeutic frequency that invites tension to release at a physical, cellular level.

Sleep & mood

Research links sound meditation with improved sleep quality, lower tension and improved mood — a felt shift that can begin in a single session.

In an observational study of 62 participants, singing-bowl sound meditation was associated with significantly reduced tension, anger, fatigue and depressed mood — with the greatest drop in tension among first-time participants.

Goldsby et al., Journal of Evidence-Based Complementary & Alternative Medicine, 2017 (UC San Diego). Read the study →

Compassion-focused approaches are likewise supported by peer-reviewed research on anxiety, PTSD and self-criticism (Gilbert, British Journal of Clinical Psychology, 2005; read the paper). Sound healing, Access Bars and Compassion Key are complementary wellness practices offered for relaxation and well-being — not a substitute for medical or psychological treatment.
