# Soundways to Health — Brand Guidelines

**Version 1.0 · 31 August 2026**
Owner: Shri Vivek · Mississauga, Ontario, Canada
Applies to: soundwaystohealth.com, all social channels, print, email, and video.

This document is written to be handed to a designer, developer, or contractor who has
never seen the brand. Where it says **LOCKED**, do not change it without the owner's
approval. Where it says **FLEXIBLE**, use judgement within the stated bounds.

## 1. Brand foundation

### 1.1 Purpose

To give over-stimulated people a reliable way back into their own nervous system —
and to be honest with them about what that will and will not fix.

### 1.2 Positioning statement

> For high-functioning, over-stimulated adults in the Greater Toronto Area — many of
> them neurodivergent — who have read enough to be sceptical and still cannot get
> their nervous system to stand down, **Soundways to Health** is the sound and
> compassion practice that tells you exactly what it can and cannot do, and then takes
> you somewhere your thinking mind cannot follow — because Shri Vivek has lived on
> both sides of it: thirty years of Himalayan practice, and his own passage through
> neurodivergence, anxiety and childhood trauma.

**Why this and not the obvious alternative.** The obvious position is speed and
permanence — "resolved in 1–3 sessions." That position is unprovable, indistinguishable
from every competitor in the category, and in Ontario it is legally exposed. Honesty is
the rarer asset and the only one that compounds. Sceptics who convert become the most
loyal clients in wellness, and they convert on candour, not on promises.

### 1.3 Brand promise

**You will be told the truth, and then you will be taken somewhere quiet.**

Operationally that means: every claim carries its limit, every session is individualised,
and every price is published before you ask.

### 1.4 The three values

**1. Candour before conversion.**
If a claim cannot survive a sceptic reading the underlying study, it does not ship.
Every efficacy statement carries its own limitation in the same breath.

**2. The practitioner is the product.**
This is not a franchise, a method, or an app. It is one person who reads what is in
front of him and shapes the session to it. Everything must feel authored by a human
who is present.

**3. Silence is the deliverable.**
Not relaxation, not vibes, not content. The output of every session — and of every
piece of communication — is a reduction in noise. If a design element, a caption, or
an animation adds noise, it fails on brand terms regardless of how well it performs.

### 1.5 Personality sliders

Mark shows the target. Do not drift more than one notch in either direction.

```
clinical      ─────────●──────────  intuitive
                       ▲ 60% intuitive — evidence-literate, not evidence-led

ancient       ────────────●───────  modern
                          ▲ 65% modern — ancient in language, contemporary in form

loud          ●───────────────────  quiet
              ▲ 95% quiet — restraint is the whole aesthetic argument

institutional ──────────────────●─  personal
                                ▲ 90% personal — one named human, always

ornate        ────●───────────────  spare
                  ▲ 80% spare — one accent, one gesture, generous space

playful       ──●─────────────────  serious
                ▲ 85% serious — warm, never jokey; gravity without gloom

mystical      ──────────●─────────  grounded
                        ▲ 60% grounded — Sanskrit is permitted, magic is not
```

### 1.6 Archetype

**Primary: Sage.** The brand explains itself. It cites, it qualifies, it teaches the
mechanism before it sells the outcome. This is the register that separates it from a
category that runs almost entirely on assertion.

**Secondary: Caregiver.** Held, not fixed. The client is never a problem to be solved;
they are a person to be accompanied. Expressed in copy through the passive-receptive
voice: *"Nothing is asked of you but to arrive."*

**The archetype to actively suppress: Magician.** Transformation-on-command language —
*permanent, resolved, removed, nullified, 1–3 sessions* — is the house style of the
entire competitive set and the single fastest way to become indistinguishable from it.
It also directly contradicts the Sage material sitting three scrolls further down the
same page. Where Magician and Sage conflict, Sage wins every time.

## 2. Audience

Described by state of mind on arrival, not demographics.

### 2.1 Primary — "The tired sceptic"

They arrive at 11pm, on a phone, in bed, after a day that did not end. They have
already tried the app, the breathing exercise, the podcast, and probably a course of
talk therapy that helped and then plateaued. They are competent at almost everything
except switching off.

They want this to work and they are braced for it to be nonsense. The first thing they
look for is the thing that will let them dismiss you — an unqualified claim, a stock
photo of a lotus, a price hidden behind a form. Give them none of those and they will
read the entire page.

**What converts them:** published prices, a named study with its limitation stated, a
practitioner with a face and a history, and an explanation of the mechanism before the
invitation to book.
**What loses them:** the word *permanent*, an unattributed statistic, a testimonial
with no source, and any suggestion that scepticism is itself a blockage.

### 2.2 Secondary — "The depleted carer"

A nurse, teacher, therapist, or parent of a child with high needs. They are not
sceptical; they are empty. They do not need the mechanism explained and will skim the
science section. They need to know it will be easy to receive, that nothing will be
asked of them, and that they can be somewhere safe without performing.

**What converts them:** *in your own home*, *fully clothed*, *nothing is asked of you*,
appointment flexibility, and evening hours.
**What loses them:** any hint of effort, homework, or a programme to commit to.

### 2.3 Tertiary — "The buyer, not the receiver"

A yoga studio owner, wellness centre programmer, or corporate HR/People lead booking
for a group. They arrive from a different direction entirely: capacity, insurance,
logistics, price per head. They are currently underserved by the site — Group Sound
Bath and Corporate Wellness are both priced "By quote" with no further path.

**What converts them:** a one-page capability summary, group size ranges, what is
required of the venue, and a direct enquiry route that is not the individual booking
calendar.

## 3. Verbal identity

### 3.1 Voice attributes

**Precise, not clinical.**
*We are:* "A gentle 32-point head session that releases the stored electromagnetic
charge of thoughts, fixed beliefs and emotions."
*We are not:* "A powerful modality that unlocks your body's innate wisdom."
Name the instrument, the count, the duration, the mechanism. Specificity is the proof
that a real practice exists behind the page.

**Honest about limits, not hedging.**
*We are:* "In an observational study of 62 participants, singing-bowl sound meditation
was associated with significantly reduced tension… "
*We are not:* "Studies show sound healing reduces stress."
State the finding and its boundary in one sentence. Never state a finding and put the
caveat in a footer — that reads as legal cover, not candour.

**Warm, not soothing.**
*We are:* "You surface slowly, wrapped in quiet. A sip of water, a few grounding words."
*We are not:* "Allow yourself to melt into a state of blissful surrender."
Warmth comes from concrete sensory detail and second person. Soothing-voice register —
*allow, simply, gently release, beautiful soul* — is category wallpaper.

**Direct, not commanding.**
*We are:* "Not sure where to start? Book an introductory session and we'll find the
right path together."
*We are not:* "Take the first step on your healing journey today!"
Offer a next action, name the uncertainty it resolves, and stop.

**Sparing with the sacred, not performative.**
*We are:* One Sanskrit anchor — *Nishabdhaa* — defined once and then used as a name.
*We are not:* Namaste, chakra-speak in every paragraph, Sanskrit as decoration.
One borrowed word carries weight. Six make it costume.

### 3.2 Tone by context

| Context | Shift |
|---|---|
| **Homepage / hero** | Most lyrical point in the system. Fragment sentences permitted. Serif-scale poetry: *"Frequency to freedom. Sound to silence."* Maximum two such lines per section. |
| **Service pages** | Explanatory. Mechanism first, benefit second, price third, invitation last. Lists over paragraphs. No lyricism inside a price block. |
| **Science section** | Flattest, most careful register in the whole brand. No metaphor. Named authors, named journals, named limitations. If a sentence here could appear in a supplement ad, rewrite it. |
| **Booking & confirmation** | Practical and reassuring. What to wear, what to bring, where to park, what happens if you are late. Logistics *are* the care here. |
| **Instagram / short-form** | Same voice, shorter breath. One idea per post. Never adopt platform voice — no "POV:", no trend audio built on irony, no engagement bait questions. |
| **An email that has to say no** | Warmest and briefest. Decline in the first sentence, give the reason in the second, offer the alternative in the third, and do not apologise more than once. Never explain at length — length reads as guilt. |
| **A distressed enquiry** | Drop all brand language. Plain sentences. State clearly that this is a complementary wellness practice, not crisis care, and give a real resource (Talk Suicide Canada 988; ConnexOntario 1-866-531-2600). Then, and only then, offer what you do. |

### 3.3 Vocabulary

**Use:** silence · stillness · Nishabdhaa · frequency · vibration · resonance ·
nervous system · parasympathetic · attunement · session · practitioner · complementary ·
individualised · associated with · studied for · researched for · in-person · received ·
held · quiet · rest · recalibrate · threshold · return.

**Never use:**

| Banned | Why | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| permanent, permanently | Unprovable; regulatory exposure | lasting, durable, in many cases enduring |
| cure, heal *(as a verb with an object)*, fix | Treatment claim | support, ease, work with |
| resolved, removed, nullified, eliminated | Outcome guarantee | shifted, released, quieter |
| treat, treatment, therapy, therapeutic *(of a session)* | Regulated-practice language | session, practice, work |
| psychotherapy, counselling, patient, client diagnosis | Protected title / controlled act in Ontario | session, practice, person you are working with |
| guaranteed, always, everyone, proven | Absolutes | often, many people, studied for |
| detox, toxins, cellular healing, quantum, DNA activation | Pseudoscience markers; destroys sceptic trust | *(delete; do not replace)* |
| energy blockage, negative energy, aura cleansing | Same | stored charge *(Access Bars' own term)*, tension, held stress |
| journey *(as a noun for the client's life)* | Category cliché | your practice, your work, this session |
| unlock, unleash, manifest, high vibration | Marketing-mystic register | *(delete)* |
| miracle, magic, transformation *(as a product noun)* | Magician archetype | change, shift |
| soul, tribe, sacred space *(as a marketing phrase)* | Performative | *(delete or make literal: "the studio")* |

**Always pair, never separate:** any efficacy claim + its limitation. Any mention of
PTSD, trauma, anxiety, or ADHD + the complementary-practice disclaimer within the same
visual block, not in a page footer.

### 3.4 Three rewrites from live site copy

**A. The Compassion Key card**

> **Before (live):** "A fast, safe and permanent process for anxiety, PTSD, fears and
> trauma — most issues resolved within 1–3 targeted sessions."

> **After:** "A guided compassion process for fear, anxiety and long-held emotional
> pain. Many people notice a clear shift within one to three sessions; some need
> longer. A complementary wellness practice, not a substitute for medical or
> psychological care."

*Fixes:* removes an unprovable permanence claim and a treatment claim about PTSD;
replaces a guarantee with an observation; moves the disclaimer to the point of claim
instead of the page footer. Keeps the speed benefit as a real, hedged observation
rather than deleting it — the honesty is the differentiator, not the absence of a
benefit.

**B. The About credibility line**

> **Before (live):** "a space for transformation trusted by psychologists and doctors,
> with over 50 five-star reviews."

> **After:** "Referred to by psychologists and physicians in the GTA, and rated 5.0
> across 50+ reviews — read them →" *(with a live link to the actual review source)*

*Fixes:* "trusted by" is unverifiable and "transformation" is Magician register. Naming
the region, giving a number with a rating, and making it clickable turns a claim into
evidence. **If the reviews cannot be linked, delete the sentence.** An unlinked review
count is exactly the detail the primary audience uses to dismiss a page.

**C. The Access Bars pull-quote**

> **Before (live):** "How much of your mind's noise are you willing to lose?"

> **After:** "How much of the noise are you still carrying because no one ever offered
> to take it?"

*Fixes:* the original is a good line built on a subtly accusatory frame — *willing*
implies the reader is the obstacle, which is the one move that loses a sceptic and
shames a depleted carer. The rewrite keeps the rhythm and the challenge but relocates
the cause outside the reader.

## 4. Logo & mark

### 4.1 Construction — **LOCKED**

The mark is a continuous **S** looping through an **o**, tracing an infinity form, with
a **bindu** (still point) at the centre of the o. It reads simultaneously as the "So"
of Soundways, as ∞, and as sound passing through silence.

Source of truth: `assets/logo-mark.svg` (viewBox `0 0 220 120`) and
`assets/logo-wordmark.svg` (viewBox `0 0 820 170`).

Geometry — do not redraw:
- **o:** circle at `cx 150, cy 60, r 40`, stroke-width `10.5`, no fill.
- **S:** single open path, stroke-width `10.5`, `stroke-linecap: round`, passing through
  the o's centre point.
- **bindu:** circle at `cx 150, cy 60, r 5`, solid fill `#F4C784`.
- Stroke weight is uniform at `10.5` across both elements. This is what makes it read as
  one continuous gesture.

Gradients — **LOCKED**:
- `#flow` (the S), horizontal: `#F4C784` at 0 → `#E8A24A` at .48 → `#5EB6C4` at 1.
- `#still` (the o), diagonal: `#5EB6C4` → `#3A97A6`.

The gradient is the brand's core idea made visual: gold (sound, movement, warmth)
resolving into teal (silence, stillness, depth). It is not decoration and must not be
flattened to a single colour except in the mono variants below.

### 4.2 Clear space

Minimum clear space on all four sides = **the radius of the o (40 units in mark space,
≈ 36% of mark height)**. Nothing enters this zone — no text, no rule, no image edge, no
photographic subject.

### 4.3 Minimum size

| Context | Minimum |
|---|---|
| Mark alone, screen | 28px tall |
| Mark alone, print | 8mm tall |
| Lockup (mark + wordmark), screen | 120px wide |
| Lockup, print | 32mm wide |
| Favicon / app icon | Use the mark only, bindu enlarged to `r 7` for legibility |

Below 28px the bindu disappears and the loop crossing turns to mud. If you need smaller,
use the o with the bindu alone.

### 4.4 Approved variants

1. **Primary lockup** — mark + "Soundways" in Cormorant Garamond 500 + "TO HEALTH" in
   Jost 400, uppercase, `letter-spacing: .3em`. On `#080B16` or on a darkened photo.
2. **Mark only** — social avatars, favicon, session-end stamps, embroidery.
3. **Horizontal lockup** — mark left, wordmark right. The default for site headers.
4. **Mono gold** — `#E8A24A` solid, both elements. For single-colour print, foil stamp,
   and any surface where gradients will band.
5. **Mono ink** — `#F3EEE4` solid on dark. For photography overlays where gold competes
   with warm image content.
6. **Mono night** — `#080B16` solid on light. The only approved light-background form.

### 4.5 Misuse — **NEVER**

- Never change the gradient direction, stops, or colours.
- Never place the mark on a mid-tone photograph without a scrim; contrast against the
  pixels directly behind the strokes must reach 4.5:1.
- Never add a drop shadow, outer glow, bevel, or outline to the mark.
- Never rotate, skew, mirror, or stretch. The infinity form is directional.
- Never separate the bindu from the o, or the S from the o.
- Never place the lockup on `#F5F0E8` or any Wix-default cream. That is the old system.
- Never recreate the wordmark in another typeface, including a "close enough" serif.
- Never put the mark inside a circle, rounded square, or badge container.
- Never animate the mark's stroke as a drawing-on effect faster than 1.2s — it reads as
  a loading spinner.
- Never use the mark as a bullet, divider, or background watermark at low opacity.

## 6. Typography system

### 6.1 Families — **LOCKED**

**Display: Cormorant Garamond** — weights 400, 500, 600; italics 300, 400, 500.
Fallback stack: `'Cormorant Garamond', Georgia, 'Times New Roman', serif`.

**Interface & body: Jost** — weights 200, 300, 400, 500, 600.
Fallback stack: `'Jost', system-ui, -apple-system, 'Segoe UI', sans-serif`.

Both from Google Fonts. Self-host if you can — see §12.

### 6.2 Type scale

Fluid via `clamp()`. Every level below is currently in the build or a consolidation of
levels that were.

### 6.3 Pairing rules

- **Cormorant never sets more than three lines.** It is a display face here. Anything
  running longer is Jost.
- **Jost never sets a headline above 32px.** It has no display presence at scale.
- **Uppercase belongs to Jost only,** and always with tracking ≥ `.18em`. Uppercase
  Cormorant is not an approved form anywhere in the system.
- **Italic Cormorant is the emphasis device** — used in the hero, in pull-quotes, and on
  the Roman numerals. Never bold Cormorant for emphasis; never italicise Jost.
- **One display level per section.** A section has a D-level or an H1, not both.
- **Maximum measure: 68 characters** for Lead, **74** for Body. Centre-aligned text
  caps at 56.
- **Never letterspace lowercase body copy.** Tracking above `.02em` is reserved for
  uppercase.

### 6.4 What each level is for

D1 exists once, in the hero, and is the brand's single loudest moment — permitted a
two-colour treatment (Ink into Gold into Teal) that appears nowhere else. D2 and H1
open sections and carry the argument. Eyebrows do the navigational work D-levels
cannot — they tell the reader where they are in a very long page. Body at 1.75–1.8
line-height is deliberate: the near-double leading is what makes a 18,000px page feel
unhurried rather than endless.

## 7. Imagery & art direction

### 7.1 Subject

**Photography only.** No illustration, no 3D render, no iconography beyond functional
UI glyphs, no abstract gradient meshes.

Three approved subjects, in priority order:

1. **The practitioner, working.** Shri Vivek mid-practice — hands on a bowl, eyes
   closed, absorbed. Never posed to camera, never smiling at the lens. He is the
   brand's primary image and should appear in the hero of any major surface.
2. **The instruments, close.** Bowl rims, gong surface, tuning forks, handpan — shot
   tight enough that texture and metal grain read. These carry the craft argument.
3. **The room.** Candlelight, salt-lamp glow, a cushion, a blanket, the threshold.
   Always empty of people. These carry the invitation.

### 7.2 Treatment

- **Grade:** warm, low-key. Lift shadows slightly so they read as deep blue-black
  (toward `#080B16`) rather than crushed neutral black. Warm the highlights toward
  Gold. Desaturate greens and cyans in the environment by roughly 20% so the brand's
  own teal stays the only cool note that reads as intentional.
- **Exposure:** underexpose by 1/3 to 2/3 stop relative to a standard grade. The brand
  lives at dusk.
- **Scrim:** any image carrying type gets a linear or radial darkening pass sufficient
  to hit 4.5:1 behind the glyphs. Build the scrim into the CSS overlay, not the JPEG,
  so the image can be reused.
- **Grain:** a light film grain is permitted and encouraged at 2–4% opacity. Digital
  clarity reads cold in this system.

### 7.3 Composition

- Subject occupies one third of frame; the rest is dark negative space where type will
  live. Shoot for the layout.
- Horizon and strong lines off-centre. Dead-centre symmetry is reserved for the ritual
  section.
- Crops favour 3:2 and 16:9 landscape for web; 4:5 and 9:16 for social. Shoot wide
  enough to crop to all four.
- Hands and faces cropped by the frame edge are encouraged — it reads as observed
  rather than staged.

### 7.4 Never

- **NEVER** stock photography of any kind. Not one image. If a real photograph does not
  exist, the section ships without an image.
- **NEVER** a lotus, mandala, chakra diagram, silhouette-in-sunset, or floating woman.
- **NEVER** a white or light-grey background.
- **NEVER** a smiling model in athleisure.
- **NEVER** the same image used twice on one page.
- **NEVER** heavy vignettes, HDR, orange-and-teal blockbuster grade, or blur used to
  disguise a low-quality source.
- **NEVER** AI-generated imagery of the practitioner, the room, or the instruments. The
  credibility of a one-person practice cannot survive a synthetic hand.

### 7.5 Three executable prompts

Written to be handed to a photographer, or to an image tool for **environment and
instrument shots only** — never for the practitioner.

> **Prompt 1 — Hero, the practitioner.**
> "Close portrait, 85mm, f/1.8. A man in his forties in a loose white cotton shirt,
> eyes closed, absorbed, striking a large Himalayan singing bowl with a wooden mallet.
> Lit by a single warm practical source low and camera-left, roughly 2700K; deep
> blue-black falloff filling the right two-thirds of frame. Subject placed left third.
> Background: greenery and a brass figure, thrown fully out of focus. Underexposed by
> 2/3 stop. Fine film grain. No eye contact, no smile to camera. Warm highlights,
> blue-black shadows. Landscape 3:2, with headroom above the subject for a headline."

> **Prompt 2 — Instrument macro.**
> "Extreme close-up, 100mm macro, f/4. The rim and inner curve of a platinum-infused
> Himalayan singing bowl. Hammer marks, tarnish and fingerprints visible — a worked
> object, not a showroom object. Single raking warm light from the upper left picking
> out metal grain; the rest of the frame falling to near-black. Shallow focus on the
> rim edge. Slight warm colour cast, greens and cyans desaturated. No props, no cloth,
> no hands. Square and 4:5 crops."

> **Prompt 3 — The threshold.**
> "Wide interior, 35mm, f/2.8, from standing eye height. A small home studio at dusk:
> a floor cushion and folded wool blanket, a Himalayan salt lamp glowing amber, three
> candles at varying heights, a gong on a stand just out of focus at the frame edge.
> Empty of people. Ambient light only, roughly 2400K, deeply underexposed with the
> corners falling to blue-black. Uncluttered — no more than five objects in frame.
> Warm, quiet, slightly imperfect. Landscape 16:9 with the left third empty for type."

## 8. Layout & space

### 8.1 Grid

- **Max content width:** `1180px` (`--maxw`). **LOCKED.**
- **12-column grid**, `24px` gutters desktop, `16px` tablet, `12px` mobile.
- **Page margins:** `clamp(20px, 5vw, 64px)`.
- **Text measure inside the grid:** Lead and Body constrained to `680px` regardless of
  column span.
- **Breakpoints:** `900px` and `560px`. Two breakpoints, not five — the fluid type scale
  does most of the adaptation.

### 8.2 Spacing scale

An 8px base with a deliberate jump at the section level. Use these tokens; do not
invent intermediate values.

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `--s-1` | `4px` | Icon-to-label, tight inline pairs |
| `--s-2` | `8px` | Within a component |
| `--s-3` | `16px` | Between related elements |
| `--s-4` | `24px` | Card padding (mobile), list item spacing |
| `--s-5` | `40px` | Card padding (desktop), element groups |
| `--s-6` | `64px` | Between component blocks |
| `--s-7` | `96px` | Sub-section separation |
| `--s-8` | `clamp(96px, 14vh, 180px)` | **Section padding, top and bottom** |

Section padding is the brand's most important spacing decision. The generosity of
`--s-8` is what produces the unhurried feeling; cutting it to save scroll length would
destroy the aesthetic argument faster than any colour change.

### 8.3 Corner radii — **REVISED**

The current build uses seven values (2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 999). Consolidate to four:

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `--r-sm` | `4px` | Tags, small chips, input fields |
| `--r-md` | `10px` | Cards, panels, images, price blocks |
| `--r-lg` | `16px` | Full-bleed feature blocks, modals |
| `--r-pill` | `999px` | Buttons and pills only |

### 8.4 Elevation

This system has almost no elevation. Depth comes from **background value shifts**
(Night → Night Deep → Panel) and from the `--line` hairline, not from shadows.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **0 — Flat** | Default. Background shift + `1px solid var(--line)`. |
| **1 — Raised** | Hover only: `box-shadow: 0 12px 40px rgba(0,0,0,.45)` + border-colour lift to `rgba(232,162,74,.28)`. |
| **2 — Overlay** | Modals, mobile nav drawer: `0 24px 80px rgba(0,0,0,.6)` + `backdrop-filter: blur(18px)`. |

**Never** a shadow on a static element. **Never** a coloured glow.

### 8.5 Rhythm rules

- Alternate `--night` and `--night2` between adjacent sections. Never two identical
  grounds in a row; never a hard rule between sections — the value change *is* the
  divider.
- Every section opens with an Eyebrow, then a display headline, then a Lead. That
  three-beat is the page's metronome and should not be broken more than once per page.
- Roman numerals (i, ii, iii, iv) mark any ordered set of three or four. This is a
  signature device — see §11.
- One full-bleed image band per three text sections, maximum.

## 9. Motion

### 9.1 Easing — **LOCKED**

```css
--ease: cubic-bezier(.2, .8, .2, 1);   /* fast out, long settle — the house curve */
--ease-in: cubic-bezier(.4, 0, 1, 1);  /* exits only */
```

One curve does nearly everything. It accelerates immediately and decelerates over a
long tail — the motion equivalent of a struck bowl. **Never** use `ease-in-out`,
`linear`, or any spring/bounce/overshoot. Overshoot is playful; this brand is not.

### 9.2 Duration

| Duration | Use |
|---|---|
| `.3s` | Colour and border-colour changes |
| `.4s` | Card and button transforms, shadows |
| `.5s` | Header state change, larger transforms, backdrop-filter |
| `.8s` | Scroll-reveal fade-in (`opacity` + `visibility`) |
| `1.2s+` | Ambient loops only — logo stroke, breathing gradients |

### 9.3 What earns animation

- Scroll-reveal: elements fade from `opacity: 0` and `translateY(16px)`. Once, never on
  scroll-back. Stagger sibling cards by `80ms`, capped at four items.
- Hover on interactive elements: `translateY(-3px)` maximum, plus a border-colour lift.
- The header's transition from transparent to blurred solid.
- The hero wave divider, if it moves at all, at ≥ 6s per cycle.

### 9.4 What does not

Parallax on more than one layer. Text animating character by character. Anything that
moves while the user is reading. Counters ticking up. Carousels that advance on their
own. Cursor followers. Scroll-jacking of any kind. Loading spinners longer than 400ms —
if it takes longer than that, the page is too heavy.

### 9.5 Reduced motion — **REQUIRED**

```css
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  *, *::before, *::after {
    animation-duration: .01ms !important;
    animation-iteration-count: 1 !important;
    transition-duration: .01ms !important;
    scroll-behavior: auto !important;
  }
}
```

Scroll-reveal elements must be **visible by default** and animated in by script, so
that a reduced-motion or no-JS visitor sees content rather than blank space.

## 10. Applications

### 10.1 Website

Night ground. Header transparent over the hero, transitioning at 80px scroll to
`rgba(8,11,22,.82)` + `backdrop-filter: blur(14px)` + a `--line` bottom border. Logo
lockup left at 120px wide; nav right in Jost 400 uppercase 12px `.18em`, Muted, with
the active item in Gold. One Gold pill CTA at the right end.

Section pattern: Eyebrow (Muted, `.3em`) → D2 or H1 (Ink, with one italic Cormorant
phrase in Gold or Teal) → Lead (Body, max 680px) → content. Section padding `--s-8`.
Ground alternates Night / Night Deep.

Cards: `--panel` fill, `1px solid var(--line)`, `--r-md`, `--s-5` padding, hover lift to
elevation 1. Price cards carry the price in Cormorant 500 at Price scale, Gold.

**One CTA per section, and it is always the same words:** *Book a session* or the
service-specific variant. Do not invent new CTA language per section.

### 10.2 Instagram grid

The grid is read as a whole before any single post is read. Plan in rows of three.

- **Ground:** every post sits on Night or on a graded brand photograph. **No white
  posts. Ever.** The feed's near-black block is the recognition asset at thumbnail size.
- **Row pattern:** photograph → type-led → photograph, repeating. Never three type-led
  posts adjacent.
- **Type-led posts:** one Cormorant line, D3 scale, centred, on Night, with a single
  Gold hairline rule beneath at 40px wide. Nothing else. No logo — the type *is* the
  logo at this scale.
- **Photograph posts:** the image graded per §7.2, with an Eyebrow-S in the lower-left
  safe area only when the image needs identification.
- **Carousels:** slide 1 is the hook in Cormorant. Slides 2–n carry Jost body at
  Body-S. Final slide is a Gold pill CTA on Night. Roman numerals mark the sequence.
- **Accent rotation by topic:** Gold for sound healing, Teal for Access Bars and any
  science post, Ember for Compassion Key. Over a nine-post grid this produces a subtle
  colour rhythm that reads as a system, not a mood.
- **Never:** a white border between posts, a text-on-photo caption in a system font, a
  meme template, or a face-to-camera thumbnail with an open mouth.

### 10.3 Booking confirmation email

Plain, warm, useful. Most email clients will strip half of any design, so the design
must survive as text.

- Night `#080B16` background with `#F3EEE4` text, and a light-mode fallback of Ink
  ground with Night text for clients that force it. Test both.
- Header: mono-ink mark at 40px, centred, with `--s-6` of space beneath.
- Subject line: `Your session is confirmed — Saturday 14 Sept, 3:00pm`
- Body, in Jost 300 at 16px/1.75:
  - One line confirming what, when, where.
  - **What to expect** — three bullets: what to wear, what to bring, what happens first.
  - **Finding us** — address, parking note, and "text me when you arrive" with the
    number.
  - **If you need to change it** — the cancellation window, stated plainly, in a
    `--panel` block with a `--line` border.
  - A closing line in Cormorant italic at 21px: *"Nothing is asked of you but to
    arrive."*
- One Gold pill button maximum, and only if there is a genuine action (add to calendar).
- Signed with the practitioner's name, not "the Soundways team." There is no team.

### 10.4 Business card

- **Stock:** uncoated, 400–450gsm, Night `#080B16` through-dyed or double-duplexed so
  the edge reads black. Soft-touch laminate is permitted; gloss is not.
- **Front:** mark only, gold foil, 22mm wide, optically centred (slightly above true
  centre). Nothing else.
- **Back:** wordmark lockup top-left in Ink foil or white ink; name and modalities in
  Jost 400 uppercase 7pt at `.18em`; phone, email and domain in Jost 300 8pt. Generous
  margins — minimum 8mm on all sides.
- Foil match to `--amber-d` `#C9822B`, which reproduces closer to `#E8A24A` under foil
  than the screen value does.
- **Never** print the gradient. Business cards use mono gold.

### 10.5 Short-form video end-card

Two seconds minimum, four maximum. 9:16.

1. Cut to Night ground on the last beat of audio — the silence is the point.
2. Mark fades in at centre over `.5s` with `--ease`, sized to 18% of frame width.
3. `SOUNDWAYSTOHEALTH.COM` in Jost 400 uppercase, `.3em`, Muted, appearing `200ms`
   after the mark, 12% below it.
4. Hold. No music sting, no whoosh, no logo animation, no "follow for more."

The end-card's job is to let the viewer land. Every competitor ends on a hook; ending
on silence is the differentiator applied to video.

## 11. Content strategy

### 11.1 The three pillars

**Pillar 1 — "What the research actually says." (≈40% of output)**
One study, one finding, one limitation, per post. Named author, named journal, named
sample size. This is the pillar nobody else in the category will run, because it
requires reading the papers and it forecloses the easy claim. It is therefore the only
pillar that builds a defensible position. It also directly serves the primary audience,
who arrive looking for a reason to trust.

*Credibility to own: the practitioner who tells you the study had 62 people and no
control group.*

**Pillar 2 — "The instruments." (≈35%)**
Craft, provenance, and physics. What a platinum-infused bowl is and why it differs.
What a gong actually does to a room's air. Hans Cousto's Cosmic Octave and the 136.10Hz
Earth tone. Don Conreaux and the origin of the gong bath. Tuning fork placement. This
pillar is inherently visual, inherently sonic, and impossible for a content-farm
competitor to fake, because it requires the objects.

*Credibility to own: the person who knows where the sound comes from.*

**Pillar 3 — "The nervous system, plainly." (≈25%)**
Why you cannot think your way out of activation. What parasympathetic actually means.
Why neurodivergent nervous systems fatigue differently. Practical, non-selling, useful
whether or not the reader ever books. Written for the tired sceptic at 11pm.

*Credibility to own: explaining the reader to themselves without diagnosing them.*

**Explicitly not a pillar:** personal transformation testimonials, client stories,
before-and-afters, daily affirmations, or anything that positions the practitioner as a
teacher of life rather than a practitioner of a craft.

### 11.2 Format by platform

| Platform | Primary format | Cadence | Pillar weighting |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Instagram feed** | Type-led single + graded photo, alternating | 3×/week | 2, 3, 1 |
| **Instagram Reels** | 20–35s single-instrument sound clips, real audio | 2×/week | 2 heavily |
| **Instagram Stories** | Studio setup, day-of availability, replies | Daily-ish | Unstructured |
| **YouTube** | 20–60 min unbroken sound baths, static or single slow shot | 1×/month | 2 |
| **Google Business Profile** | Photos + review responses + posts | Weekly | 1, 2 |
| **Email** | One long-form letter, no template | Monthly | 1, 3 |
| **Website** | Long-form service and science pages | Quarterly revision | All |

Note that **Google Business Profile is doing more work than any social channel** for a
local, in-person, appointment-based service. It is currently the most under-invested
surface in the entire brand.

### 11.3 The one distribution idea most likely to travel

**"The 62 People Study" — a recurring, numbered series that debunks the sound healing
industry using its own citations.**

Each entry takes a claim the wellness industry makes constantly — *sound healing cures
anxiety, 528Hz repairs DNA, gong baths detoxify* — traces it to its actual source, and
reports what the source really says. Some entries will support the claim partially.
Some will demolish it. The practitioner running the series *sells the thing being
examined*, and that is precisely why it travels: it is the last thing the audience
expects, and it is structurally screenshot-able.

Why it works where a sound clip does not: a 30-second bowl video competes against ten
thousand identical bowl videos and is judged on audio quality alone. A practitioner
publicly disproving a claim in his own industry has no competition, generates comment
sections that argue with each other, and — critically — gets shared *by sceptics*, who
are the exact primary audience and the hardest people to reach with wellness marketing.
It converts the brand's single biggest liability (a category full of nonsense) into its
distribution engine.

**Guardrails:** never name or target a competitor. The subject is always the claim, never
a person. Every entry ends with what the evidence *does* support, so the series builds
rather than corrodes.

### 11.4 The unmistakable signature

With the logo cropped out, a piece is recognisable as Soundways to Health by four
things working together:

1. **A near-black blue-cast ground at ~85% of the surface, broken by exactly one gold
   element.** The discipline of a single accent against that much darkness is the
   fastest visual identifier.
2. **The two-beat transformation line** — `Frequency to freedom.` `Fear to love.`
   `32 points. One profound letting-go.` Two short clauses, a hard stop between them,
   the second shorter than the first. This is the brand's verbal fingerprint and it
   should appear at least once in every substantial piece.
3. **Roman numerals as sequence markers** — i, ii, iii, iv — set in italic Cormorant at
   display scale, in Gold or Teal. Nobody else in the category does this.
4. **A claim immediately followed by its own limit,** in the same block, in the same
   voice. The verbal equivalent of showing your working.

If three of those four are present, the piece is on brand. If fewer than two are, it is
not, regardless of how well it uses the palette.

## 12. Governance

### 12.1 Locked — requires owner approval to change

- The logo mark geometry, the two gradients, and the bindu.
- Gold `#E8A24A` as the sole primary accent.
- Night `#080B16` as the primary ground; Ink `#F3EEE4` as the type white.
- Cormorant Garamond + Jost as the type pairing.
- `cubic-bezier(.2, .8, .2, 1)` as the house easing curve.
- The banned-words list in §3.3 — this one is a compliance matter, not a style
  preference.
- Photography only, no stock, no AI imagery of people or the practice.
- `1180px` max content width.

### 12.2 Flexible — use judgement

- Section order and length on any page.
- Which of Teal or Ember accents a given non-service piece.
- Exact spacing within a component, provided it uses scale tokens.
- Social crop ratios, caption length, hashtag strategy.
- Adding type scale levels *between* existing ones, if a real need appears.
- Illustration and diagram style for the science pillar — this is genuinely undefined
  and the next thing worth designing.

### 12.3 The decision rule

When this document does not cover your case, apply these three questions in order:

1. **Does it add noise?** The product is silence. Anything that adds visual, verbal, or
   motion noise fails, no matter how well it performs. If in doubt, remove it.
2. **Could a sceptic use it to dismiss us?** Read the element as the tired sceptic at
   11pm looking for a reason to close the tab. Unsourced numbers, absolutes, stock
   imagery, and hidden prices all fail this test.
3. **Does it sound like one person, or like a brand?** It must sound like one person.
   If the sentence could have been written by a marketing department, rewrite it or
   delete it.

If those three do not resolve it, choose the quieter option and flag it for the next
revision of this document.

### 12.4 Compliance floor — not negotiable

Ontario regulates "psychotherapist" as a protected title, and the practice of
psychotherapy is a controlled act under the *Regulated Health Professions Act*. This
practice does not hold that registration. Therefore, on every surface, permanently:

- Never use the words *psychotherapy, therapy, therapist, treatment, patient, diagnose,*
  or *cure* to describe this work.
- Never state or imply that a condition — PTSD, anxiety, depression, ADHD, trauma — will
  be treated, resolved, cured, or permanently removed.
- Always pair any mention of a clinical condition with the complementary-practice
  disclaimer, in the same visual block.
- Always describe outcomes as observed and variable: *many people notice*, *often*,
  *in some cases*.
- Maintain a visible, standing disclaimer on every page that mentions a health outcome.

This floor also governs paid advertising: the current "fast, safe and permanent process
for anxiety, PTSD, fears and trauma" wording will fail Meta's health-claims review and
blocks any paid launch until it is changed.
